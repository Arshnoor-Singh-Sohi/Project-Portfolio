## How to use this page

1. Start with "Read these first". It lists ten sources in the order to study them.
2. The topic sections list the sources of the whole course. Each entry gives the full citation, one line on why it matters, and the course pages that use it.
3. Page codes: "Part 2: L1" is lesson 1 of Part 2. In Part 6, P1 to P13 are the paper pages. In Part 9, P1 to P9 are the projects.
4. **UNVERIFIED** means that a detail could not be confirmed from a primary source. Do not teach it as fact. "Venue not confirmed" means that the paper exists, but its place of publication was not confirmed.
5. Author lists give all authors when there are six or fewer, and otherwise the first three and "et al.". Some entries give initials only, because only the initials were checked.

## Read these first

Study these ten sources in this order. Each one builds on the one before.

1. **Good (1965)**. Irving John Good. "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine". Advances in Computers, Volume 6, pages 31–88. 1965. https://www.Gwern.net/doc/ai/1966-good.pdf. *Why first:* Section 2 is the origin of the "intelligence explosion" argument. Read only that section, and note the condition in its "last invention" sentence.
2. **Chen, Wang and Qu (2026)**. Mingguang Chen, Licheng Wang, Bo Qu. "Recursive Self-Improvement in AI: From Bounded Self-Refinement to Autonomous Research Loops". 2026. arXiv:2607.07663. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07663. *Why second:* a map of 1,250 papers, and the cut between bounded self-refinement and open-ended RSI.
3. **Huang et al. (2023)**. Jie Huang, Xinyun Chen, Swaroop Mishra, et al. "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet". ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.01798. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798. *Why third:* the clearest evidence that a model that judges itself is not a reliable improvement engine.
4. **Zelikman et al. (2023), STOP**. Eric Zelikman, Eliana Lorch, Lester Mackey, Adam Tauman Kalai. "Self-Taught Optimizer (STOP): Recursively Self-Improving Code Generation". COLM 2024. arXiv:2310.02304. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02304. *Why fourth:* the smallest real case of an improver that improves itself, with its first measured safety failures.
5. **Hu, Lu and Clune (2024), ADAS**. Shengran Hu, Cong Lu, Jeff Clune. "Automated Design of Agentic Systems". ICLR 2025. arXiv:2408.08435. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08435. *Why fifth:* the step from improving prompts to designing whole agents as code, with a fixed meta agent.
6. **Zhang et al. (2025), DGM**. Jenny Zhang, Shengran Hu, Cong Lu, Robert Lange, Jeff Clune. "Darwin Gödel Machine: Open-Ended Evolution of Self-Improving Agents". ICLR 2026. arXiv:2505.22954. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954. *Why sixth:* the central system of the course, with an archive, ablations, transfer and a documented case of objective hacking.
7. **Wang et al. (2025), HGM**. Wenyi Wang, Piotr Piękos, Li Nanbo, et al. "Huxley-Gödel Machine: Human-Level Coding Agent Development by an Approximation of the Optimal Self-Improving Machine". ICLR 2026. arXiv:2510.21614. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21614. *Why seventh:* it measures that an agent's score is a weak guide to its descendants.
8. **Manheim and Garrabrant (2018)**. D. Manheim, S. Garrabrant. "Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law". 2018. arXiv:1803.04585. https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585. *Why eighth:* four ways in which a measured score parts from the real goal under optimization.
9. **Miller (2024)**. E. Miller. "Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations". 2024. arXiv:2411.00640. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640. *Why ninth:* the statistics you need to judge every number in this field.
10. **Anthropic Institute (2026)**. Marina Favaro, Phillie Wright. "Measurements for understanding the pace of AI development inside frontier labs". 2026. https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development. *Why last:* how a frontier lab measures its distance to RSI, with the AL0 to AL5 scale.
